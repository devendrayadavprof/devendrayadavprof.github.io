About

About me

I build hydrogen reactors, from the first model to the laboratory bench. At Technion I am developing a compact autothermal stacked-plate membrane reactor that converts methanol into pure hydrogen at the point of use, on board a vehicle or on site. My work spans multiphysics modeling, CAD design, fabrication of the real stack and its experimental testing.

I came to hydrogen from heat transfer. My M.Tech at IIT (ISM) Dhanbad was an experimental study of nanofluid coolants, for which I built my own measurement setup. My Ph.D. at Xi'an Jiaotong University focused on methanol steam reforming microreactors. I also spent four years as an Assistant Professor of Mechanical Engineering, where I designed and built thermal test rigs with my students.

Research

Current and past work

Postdoc2024 – present

Autothermal stacked-plate membrane reactor for onboard hydrogen production

Hydrogen is difficult to store and transport, but methanol is a liquid that today's fuel infrastructure already handles. This reactor turns methanol into fuel-cell-grade hydrogen where it is needed, in a unit compact enough for a vehicle. The concept is autothermal: burning the reactor's own off-gas supplies the heat for reforming, while a membrane separates pure hydrogen inside the same stack.

I designed and fabricated the membrane reactor stack, including the membrane support, sealing and thermal integration, and I am conducting pressure-integrity and hydrogen permeation tests on it. Multiphysics modeling of the coupled reaction, heat and mass transfer drives the development of the autothermal system. My recent paper in the Journal of Industrial and Engineering Chemistry shows how mass transport limits the performance of plate-type membrane reactors.

Ph.D.2020 – 2024

Methanol steam reforming microreactors

Microreactors make hydrogen production small, fast and controllable, but the evaporator that feeds them is often the weak link. I developed and modeled a coupled micro-evaporator and micro-reformer, and showed that a helical spiral channel design gives the best methanol conversion and efficiency. The work also produced two review papers in the Journal of Power Sources covering experimental and numerical progress in the field.

M.Tech2016 – 2018

Nanofluids as next-generation coolants

Conventional coolants such as water and ethylene glycol limit how much heat a heat exchanger or an electronics cooling system can remove. Adding nanoparticles raises thermal conductivity, but it also raises viscosity and therefore pumping power. My project asked whether that trade-off pays off.

I prepared CeO₂, Al₂O₃ and carbon nanotube nanofluids and verified their stability. I built a transient hot-wire setup to measure their thermal conductivity, and measured viscosity and rheology over a wide range of temperatures and concentrations. From these data I trained neural network models that predict viscosity and thermal conductivity, so that a coolant can be evaluated without repeating every experiment.
